## Provider-of-record questions

Get written answers before enabling live service:

1. What contractual role does Streetwise hold: agent, retailer, reseller, MVNO, MVNE customer, or another category?
2. Who is the telecommunications provider of record to the end customer?
3. Who holds the underlying carrier and spectrum authority?
4. Does the contract permit recurring U.S. domestic use?
5. Does it permit residential and commercial resale?
6. Which U.S. networks are available and what disclosure language is required?
7. Who is responsible for FCC Form 499/USAC reporting and contributions?
8. Who is responsible for Nevada PUCN or other state telecom registrations?
9. Who collects and remits federal, state and local telecom taxes/surcharges?
10. If voice is offered, who handles E911 provisioning, address validation, routing, notices and service limitations?
11. Who handles phone-number assignment, inventory, porting, port disputes and number release at termination?
12. Who handles robocall mitigation, caller-ID authentication and voice-abuse obligations where applicable?
13. Who handles lawful-intercept, sanctions, fraud, KYC and telecom-abuse obligations?
14. What SIM-swap, port-out and line-security controls/events are available?
15. What customer identity or authorisation evidence is required for number changes, SIM swaps and porting?
16. What refund, failed-activation, outage and replacement obligations apply?
17. What support/SLA and escalation model applies?
18. What happens to active lines, numbers and customer data if the provider agreement terminates?
19. What international/roaming countries may Streetwise legally sell into?
20. What data-retention, security and breach-notification duties are allocated to each party?

## Cellular-feature launch gates

### Data/hotspot

Before sale:

- provider product mapped
- data allowance and throttling documented
- hotspot/tethering terms documented
- recurring U.S. use confirmed
- taxes/surcharges resolved
- economics passed

### Voice/SMS

Before sale:

- native voice/SMS capability contractually confirmed
- provider-of-record allocation documented
- E911 responsibilities documented
- applicable voice/messaging compliance responsibilities documented
- test calls/messages complete
- customer disclosures complete

### Phone numbers and portability

Before sale:

- number assignment capability confirmed
- porting process, authorisation and SLA documented
- ownership/control of numbers documented
- SIM-swap/port-out security workflow tested
- number release/termination process documented

### Security features

Before advertising:

- feature technically implemented
- provider dependency documented
- customer controls and recovery workflow tested
- support/escalation process documented
- marketing language describes the actual protection and its limits

### Multi-network / resilience

Before advertising:

- exact networks/provider profiles documented
- device compatibility requirements documented
- manual vs automatic switching accurately described
- outage/failover/recovery tested
- no unsupported "always connected" or guaranteed-coverage claim

### Emergency connectivity reserve

Before advertising:

- provider-supported implementation chosen
- allowance/speed/duration defined
- billing/eligibility behaviour tested
- limitations disclosed
- no implication that the feature substitutes for emergency services

## Initial launch position

Streetwise should remain a software/customer-experience and cellular-service brand using licensed wholesale network infrastructure. The company should not claim to be a facilities-based nationwide carrier, own spectrum, operate towers, or control network capabilities that belong to the upstream provider.

The business can still operate as a genuine cellular brand through an MVNO/MVNE/Telco-as-a-Service relationship, but the exact regulatory allocation must be determined from the signed provider model rather than guessed from marketing terminology.
