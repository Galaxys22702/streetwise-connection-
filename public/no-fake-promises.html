Streetwise commitment

No Fake Promises

Streetwise will separate what is live and verified from what is still a target, test, negotiation or future feature.

The commitment

Streetwise will not present a feature as available merely because it appears in a roadmap, exists in code, is technically possible, or is offered by a company Streetwise is evaluating. Public claims should match the service Streetwise is actually authorised and technically able to provide.

Provider relationships

Evaluating a carrier, MVNO platform, MVNE, eSIM provider or wholesale network does not make Streetwise an authorised partner of that company. Streetwise will not describe itself as powered by, partnered with, reselling or officially affiliated with a provider until the applicable rights have been granted in writing and the relationship supports the specific claim.

Prices and launch targets

Planning prices may be published while Streetwise evaluates wholesale economics, but they must be labelled as targets rather than active offers. A target price is not a promise that the final commercial plan will launch at that exact amount. Final pricing must account for wholesale connectivity, voice or messaging costs where applicable, taxes, surcharges, payment fees, support, fraud exposure and operating margin.

Data, hotspot and throttling claims

Streetwise will not advertise a specific high-speed data allowance, hotspot allowance, throttle speed, priority level or “unlimited” condition as final until the selected wholesale provider permits it and the behaviour has been mapped and tested. If a plan later uses the word “unlimited”, the material high-speed and network-management limits will be disclosed with it.

Coverage and speed

Coverage maps, network access and expected technology such as 4G or 5G do not guarantee a particular speed or signal at every address. Streetwise will avoid presenting theoretical network capability as a guaranteed customer result. Device capability, location, buildings, terrain, congestion and network operations can change real-world performance.

Multi-network and automatic switching

Streetwise is interested in multi-network resilience and future automatic switching, but those features remain provider-dependent. They will not be advertised as live until Streetwise has the necessary commercial rights, supported provisioning model and verified customer experience.

Voice, SMS and phone-number features

Streetwise will distinguish data-only connectivity from conventional mobile-phone service. Voice, SMS, Wi-Fi calling, local numbers, voicemail and number porting will only be listed as included when they are supported by the selected service and have been tested.

Security claims

Streetwise will not claim that an account, eSIM, payment system or network is impossible to compromise. Security claims should describe actual controls and processes rather than absolute guarantees. Planned protections will remain labelled as planned until implemented and verified.

International and roaming claims

International coverage, domestic roaming and travel connectivity can have different provider rules. Streetwise will identify which product a roaming claim applies to rather than implying that every domestic plan automatically works everywhere.

Roadmap versus live service

Streetwise may publicly describe what it is building toward. Roadmap items will be treated as direction, not as a contractual promise that a feature will launch on a particular date. Provider approval, regulation, testing, economics and technical feasibility can change the final implementation.

Corrections

If Streetwise discovers that a public statement is materially inaccurate or has become outdated, the intended response is to correct the public description rather than continue repeating it because it sounds better for marketing.

Current status

Streetwise is currently operating a launch waitlist and independent mobile-setup services. Public cellular sales, live Streetwise eSIM orders and paid Streetwise line activations remain disabled. The plan prices and allowances shown on the main site are negotiation targets until a wholesale agreement and testing support final offers.

What “No Fake Promises” means in practice

If Streetwise does not yet know a final answer, the site should say that the detail is pending rather than inventing certainty. The goal is not to make the service sound perfect. The goal is to make the service understandable and credible.
